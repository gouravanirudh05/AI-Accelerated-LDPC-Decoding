What This Study Set Out to Do

  • Start from NVIDIA's paper: Gong, Cammerer & Renes, Graph Neural Networks for Enhanced Decoding of Quantum LDPC Codes (2023)
  • Their decoder is a hybrid: belief propagation (BP) runs, a GNN corrects what BP is stuck on, BP runs again
  • Adapt it from quantum codes to classical LDPC codes and 5G NR LDPC codes
  • Compare against the strongest tuned baselines, not weak ones
  • Measure error rate, latency, GPU power, energy and memory
  • Run everything on Kaggle T4 GPUs, on standard codes from the literature

LDPC Codes and Belief Propagation

  • A codeword must satisfy every parity check (square): the XOR of its connected bits is 0
  • The receiver gets noisy values. Each bit starts with a confidence, its LLR: positive means "probably 0"
  • BP passes messages along the edges, updating each bit's belief from its checks
  • It stops early once all checks are satisfied

Toy code: 12 bits, 6 checks. The all-zero codeword is sent. Red bits are currently decoded wrong.

Where BP Fails

  • Waterfall: at low SNR there is simply too much noise. No decoder can fix that cheaply
  • Error floor: at high SNR, BP sometimes gets stuck on small sub-graphs (trapping sets, short cycles) and never converges
  • These stuck frames are rare but structured. A learned model can recognise the pattern and nudge BP out
  • That is the target of BP+GNN: fix BP's failures, keep BP for everything else

The BP+GNN Hybrid Decoder

Overview
Five BP runs of 10 iterations each, with a GNN between consecutive runs. Total budget: 50 BP iterations, the same as BP(50). Click any box or press → to walk through it.
BP stage
Plain sum-product BP for 10 iterations, starting from the current prior. If every parity check is satisfied, the frame is finished and leaves the batch (early stopping). Most frames never reach a GNN.
GNN stage
Only for frames BP could not solve. The GNN reads BP's state: channel LLR, current prior, BP's output belief, the edge messages and which checks fail. It runs 2 message-passing layers on the Tanner graph and outputs a correction: new prior = channel LLR + correction. Then BP restarts from that prior.
Training
End to end: gradients flow through all BP runs into every GNN stage. Loss = bit cross-entropy after every BP stage, plus a soft parity-check penalty. The model is picked on a fixed set of real BP failures.
Hard examples
Random frames at high SNR are almost all easy. So training batches are 75% frames that BP(10) failed on, collected in pools per Eb/N0 band, plus 25% random frames.

The Predecessor: GNN Only

Cammerer et al., 2022 · GNN only
  • A GNN replaces BP completely: 10 learned message-passing iterations
  • It must learn parity-check logic from scratch
  • Every iteration runs neural layers on every frame
Gong et al., 2023 · BP + GNN
  • BP does the heavy lifting. The GNN only corrects BP's stuck frames
  • The GNN starts from BP's beliefs, so it learns a much easier job
  • Neural layers run only on the few frames BP cannot solve

Both were implemented and trained here, so the comparison is direct.

OSD: ordered statistics decoding
  1. Sort the bits by reliability |LLR|
  2. Trust the k most reliable independent bits, re-encode the rest from them
  3. OSD-2: also try flipping 1 or 2 of the less reliable ones; keep the codeword closest to what was received

Strong but expensive per failed frame. A learned decoder can also be followed by OSD.

Measurement rules
Error rate: Monte Carlo, BPSK over AWGN, random codewords. Each point stops at 100 frame errors, 0.5–3 M frames, or 4–5 minutes.
Training band: per code, set from where BP(10) still fails in the baseline runs.
Cost: GPU latency at batch 1 and 64–4096, throughput, NVML power and energy per codeword, peak GPU memory.
Fairness: every decoder sees the same SNR points and the same profiling.

Tuning Round 2: Train on BP(50) Failures Instead?

Idea: on 5G most BP(10) failures just converge slowly. Train only on failures that 50 iterations cannot fix.
  • Both round-2 versions are worse than the round-1 winner, on both codes
  • BP(50) failures are very rare, so the hard-example pools are thin and the model sees little variety
  • Kept: round-1 winner (BP(10) failures, low-SNR band)
  • On 5G, no version of BP-GNN beat BP(50) in tuning. The final 5G runs use the best one anyway

Why GNN-Only Fails on 5G

  • 5G never transmits its first 2Z information bits (punctured), so their LLR is exactly 0
  • In these 5G graphs every check touches a punctured bit. Every check's soft parity signal starts at 0
  • A fresh GNN sits at a flat point: gradients ≈ 1e-5, vs ≈ 1e-1 on MacKay codes. It never moves

GNN layers keep 48 hidden values per edge, while neural BP keeps 1. That is why BP-GNN and GNN-only need roughly 5–17× the memory of neural BP. Tesla T4: 15 GB.

Conclusions

What holds up
  • Best error rate of everything tested. BP-GNN 5×40 + OSD-2 is lowest on all 10 codes: 1.6–2.8× below BP+OSD-2 on classical codes
  • Targets the error floor. On MacKay codes the gain over BP(50) grows from 1.3× to 4.6× as SNR rises
  • Cheap on average. Same average iterations as BP. About 1.5–2.3× BP(50)'s energy with early stopping
  • The hybrid is necessary. GNN-only is 10–1000× worse and uses 7–35× the energy
  • Fixed model size. 22.5k parameters on every code; neural BP grows to 290k
Limits
  • Gain is at most about 0.3 dB, often under 0.2 dB
  • No gain on 5G NR or on CCSDS without OSD; on longer 5G codes BP-GNN 5×10 is worse than BP(50)
  • Memory: 5–17× neural BP; training 4× longer
  • Neural BP is a cheap, close competitor
  • Larger codes (WiMAX, 5G BG1) had not converged after 80 minutes of training
The one-line result

A BP+GNN hybrid trained only on BP's failures gives the lowest error rate of every decoder tested on standard short LDPC codes. The gain grows toward the error floor at about 1.5–2× BP's energy, while a GNN-only decoder does not work. It does not help on 5G NR.
